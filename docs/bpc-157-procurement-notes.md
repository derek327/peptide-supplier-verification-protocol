# BPC-157 Research Procurement Notes

BPC-157 (body protection compound) is a 15-amino-acid synthetic peptide widely
used in preclinical soft-tissue research. These notes cover the
procurement-side documentation checks — not biological claims.

## 1. Molecule and form

| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| Sequence | 15 amino acids, linear, synthetic |
| Common forms | Lyophilized powder; acetate salt is common; some suppliers offer it with stabilizers — request unstabilized where your protocol requires it |
| Appearance | White to off-white lyophilized cake/powder |
| Typical vial sizes | 1–10 mg research vials |

## 2. Documentation to request

- **Identity by mass spectrometry** — BPC-157 is short, so an intact-mass
  confirmation is cheap for the supplier and non-negotiable for you. A CoA
  with only retention-time identity is not sufficient for research use.
- **Purity with stated method** — ≥ 95% is a common research baseline; the
  HPLC method (column, gradient, 214–220 nm detection) must be on the CoA.
- **Counterion / peptide content** — acetate salt mass ≠ peptide mass; get
  the peptide content % if you prepare molar stock solutions.
- **Stability statement** — storage and reconstitution guidance per lot.

## 3. Short-peptide specifics

Short peptides (≤ 20 residues) are comparatively easy to synthesize, which
means supply is fragmented and supplier QC varies widely. Consequences for
procurement:

1. **Impurity profiles matter more than the headline purity** — two lots at
   "98%" can differ in which 2% of impurities is present. Ask for the
   chromatogram when the experiment is sensitive.
2. **Oxidation is a real risk** — methionine-containing peptides oxidize;
   check whether the CoA or stability statement addresses storage under
   inert/reduced conditions where applicable.
3. **Counterfeit lot risk is elevated in popular research peptides** — the
   vial-lot-vs-CoA-lot match check (see `coa-field-guide.md`) is not
   optional for BPC-157 given the grey-market volume.

## 4. Checklist before ordering

- [ ] LC-MS identity on a lot-specific CoA
- [ ] HPLC method stated, 214–220 nm detection
- [ ] Vial lot number will match the CoA lot
- [ ] Peptide content % available
- [ ] Supplier names the testing lab (in-house or third-party)
- [ ] Reconstitution/storage guidance included
