# CoA Field Guide — What to Verify on a Certificate of Analysis

A Certificate of Analysis (CoA) is the primary documentary evidence that a
research peptide lot meets its stated specification. This guide breaks down
each field on a typical CoA and what a buyer should check before accepting it.

## 1. Lot identity

The most commonly missed check: the CoA must be for the **exact lot** being
shipped, not for a "representative" or "batch range" sample.

| Field | What to check |
|---|---|
| Lot number | Must match the vial label you receive |
| Manufacturing date | Should be recent relative to your order (peptides are not an aged product) |
| Expiry / retest date | Retest date is the useful one — expiry dates on lyophilized peptides are often conservative |

## 2. Analytical results

| Parameter | Meaning | Typical research-grade expectation |
|---|---|---|
| Purity (HPLC area %) | Main peak area as a percentage of total | ≥ 95%; many labs state ≥ 98% |
| Identity | Mass confirmation (LC-MS/ESI) that the molecule is what is claimed | Must state the method, not just "pass" |
| Counterion content | Peptide salts (acetate/TFA) affect net peptide weight | Look for a stated peptide content % |
| Water content (KF) | Residual moisture in lyophilized cake | Usually stated ≤ 5% |
| Residual solvents | Only relevant if a solvent was used in the final step | Often "N/A" for standard SPPS products |

## 3. Method statements

A CoA is only as good as its method section. Check that:

- The HPLC method (column type, mobile phase, gradient, detection wavelength)
  is stated — a CoA without a method is a certificate of nothing.
- The identity method is **mass spectrometry**, not a visual or retention-time
  match alone.
- The lab is named (in-house or third-party). Anonymous "certified by our QC"
  statements are a red flag in this industry.

## 4. Document integrity

- Does the CoA carry a document number and a date?
- Is there a signature / electronic authorization block?
- Is the layout consistent with the supplier's other lots? (A one-off
  differently-formatted CoA for a single lot is a common counterfeit pattern.)

## 5. Practical acceptance flow

1. Request the CoA **before** payment, for the exact lot number.
2. On arrival, compare the lot number on the vial to the CoA.
3. Confirm purity ≥ your internal threshold (95% is a common minimum for
   research use; 98%+ is typical for in vivo dosing protocols).
4. Keep the CoA with the lot in your documentation folder — GLP-style
   traceability starts with the certificate, not the invoice.
