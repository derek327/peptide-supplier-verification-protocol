# HPLC Purity Method Notes for Research Peptide Lots

Understanding the chromatography behind a purity number lets a buyer judge
whether a CoA purity figure is meaningful — or cosmetic.

## Why the method matters more than the number

Two suppliers can both report "≥ 98% HPLC purity" while running methods with
very different resolving power. The purity percentage is an area-%
calculation relative to **everything the detector sees**. A method that fails
to resolve a truncated peptide fragment will report a higher purity than the
lot actually contains.

## What a defensible method looks like

For typical 5–45 residue research peptides (C18 reversed-phase):

| Parameter | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Column | C18, 3–5 µm, 150–250 mm | Longer columns resolve closely-eluting impurities |
| Mobile phase | Water/ACN + 0.05–0.1% TFA | TFA is standard for peptide separations |
| Gradient | 5–80% B over 20–40 min | Shallow gradients separate impurities from main peak |
| Detection | UV 214–220 nm | Peptide bond absorbance; 280 nm alone is not peptide-typical |
| Flow | 0.8–1.0 mL/min | Consistent with column dimensions |

## What to check on the CoA method block

1. **Detection wavelength** — if the CoA lists 280 nm only, the purity figure
   may exclude non-aromatic impurities. 214–220 nm is the peptide standard.
2. **Gradient depth** — a fast 5-minute method cannot resolve complex impurity
   profiles. 20+ minute methods are the norm for QC-grade certificates.
3. **Area normalization basis** — check whether the % is normalized to the main
   peak only (common, optimistic) or to total peak area including impurities
   (stricter).
4. **TFA / acetate counterion** — net peptide content differs from gross mass;
   ask for peptide content % when dosing accuracy matters.

## Red flags in practice

- Purity stated to three decimals with no method or chromatogram attached.
- A "certificate" that is an email quote rather than a dated document.
- The same chromatogram image reused across multiple lot numbers (worth
  checking if you order multiple lots over time).

## Bottom line

A purity number without a stated method is marketing. A purity number with a
stated 214 nm, 20+ minute, C18 method — on a lot-specific, dated CoA — is
evidence. That distinction is the whole point of a verification protocol.
