# Lyophilized Peptide Handling & Stability Notes

Practical notes on receiving, storing, and reconstituting lyophilized
research peptides — written from a laboratory documentation perspective.

## 1. What the lyophilized state should look like

A properly lyophilized (freeze-dried) peptide is a **solid cake or powder**:

- White to off-white
- Adherent to the vial wall or as a loose powder
- **No liquid, no collapse, no discoloration**

A collapsed cake, sticky residue, or visible moisture is evidence the
freeze-dried state was broken — most commonly by moisture ingress during
storage or transit.

## 2. Storage conditions (lyophilized)

| Parameter | Common supplier recommendation |
|---|---|
| Temperature | -20 °C (long-term) is typical; short-term ambient for intact sealed vials is often tolerated but not recommended |
| Moisture | Store desiccated (silica gel / desiccant pouch) |
| Light | Protect from direct light — amber or foil-wrapped vials where supplied |
| Handling | Warm sealed vial to room temperature before opening to avoid condensing moisture onto the cake |

## 3. Reconstitution

- Reconstitute with the buffer and volume specified in **your** protocol,
  not the supplier's marketing sheet.
- Water for injection / sterile water is common for stock; dilute further in
  the assay buffer.
- Gentle swirling or inversion — do not vortex lyophilized cakes aggressively
  (foaming denatures surface peptide).

## 4. Post-reconstitution stability

Reconstituted peptides are generally **far less stable** than the lyophilized
cake:

- Aliquot immediately into single-use volumes.
- Store aliquots frozen (-20 °C or -80 °C per protocol).
- Avoid repeated freeze-thaw: each cycle can degrade the peptide; most labs
  accept one thaw per aliquot.
- Record the reconstitution date and aliquot volume on the tube — downstream
  concentration errors are documentation errors.

## 5. Documentation trail worth keeping

For GLP-adjacent work, per lot retain:

1. CoA (lot-specific) — see `coa-field-guide.md` in this repository.
2. Receipt check record (cake intact? lot match?).
3. Storage log (location, temperature, any temperature excursions).
4. Reconstitution log (date, buffer, volume, aliquot plan).

## 6. Stability red flags when comparing suppliers

- A supplier unwilling to state storage/stability recommendations on the CoA
  or data sheet.
- Vials without desiccant in multi-vial shipments.
- Reconstitution instructions that recommend room-temperature storage of the
  reconstituted solution for extended periods (incompatible with most peptide
  stability data).
